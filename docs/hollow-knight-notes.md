# What Makes Hollow Knight Fun — Research Report for The Barbican

Research based on design analyses (SUPERJUMP, GMTK/Boss Keys, GameDev.net, Medium design essays), Team Cherry interviews (ACMI, Nintendo AU, GamesRadar, Source Gaming), Silksong reviews (2025), and community criticism threads. Findings are organized by the six research questions, then distilled into the ranked list.

---

## Research findings

### 1. Exploration design — lost-but-curious
The defining insight (from [SUPERJUMP's "Getting Lost (by Design)"](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/getting-lost-by-design-in-hollow-knight/)) is that Hollow Knight **decomposes navigation into purchasable, player-chosen components**: you start with no map, find Cornifer per area (guided by paper scraps and audible humming — diegetic breadcrumbs), buy a partial map, buy the quill to record your own exploration, buy a compass charm to see your position — and it costs a charm slot. Each player "walks their own customized tightrope" between guided and blind. Two other load-bearing details: **map progress survives death even when currency doesn't** ("the challenge remains, but now you understand the layout"), and **the map only updates at benches**, so between rests you navigate by memory and landmarks. Cornifer's line — "getting lost and finding your way again is a pleasure like no other" — is the design thesis stated in-fiction. Team Cherry's stated stance: "the real world generally underestimates players… you can guide them a little bit, and that's enough" ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/action/hollow-knight-silksong-devs-believe-the-real-world-generally-underestimates-players-but-team-cherry-trusts-fans-to-engage-and-figure-the-metroidvania-out-you-can-guide-them-a-little-bit-and-thats-enough/)). Getting lost is fun rather than frustrating because (a) wrong turns almost always pay something — geo, a charm, a lore tablet, a shortcut lever; (b) the world loops back on itself so a wrong turn often becomes a discovered connection; (c) danger while lost is real but recoverable.

### 2. World structure
GMTK's [Boss Keys episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7ITtPPE-pXE) shows Hallownest as an unusually **dense dependency graph with many valid orders** — after the first couple of areas, 3–4 areas are open at once, and sequence breaks are tolerated, even embraced. Rules Team Cherry visibly followed: every area has a **single unmistakable identity** (one palette, one music theme, one enemy family — "eerie sparse chimes of Crystal Peak, ecclesiastical organs of Soul Sanctum" per the [soundtrack analyses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Hollow_Knight)); areas connect at **multiple points**, and one-way drops/lockable shortcuts convert long routes into short ones as you learn them; **secrets are physically embedded** (breakable walls with subtly different texture, shimmer-walls you walk through) and never appear on the map, so map-reading can't replace looking at the world ([GMTK on Silksong](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-world-design-of-hollow-knight)). Team Cherry designs "less as a series of levels, more as the building out of space" — space first, mechanics fill in ([ACMI interview](https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/from-ludum-dare-to-pharloom/)).

### 3. Movement & combat feel
The nail system's genius is that **offense, defense, and traversal are one verb**: pogo (down-slash bounce) is simultaneously an attack, an escape, and a platforming tool; the **soul meter fills only by hitting things**, so healing requires aggression — a risk/reward loop ([Medium combat analysis](https://medium.com/@roryhoeschen/the-hypnotizing-simplicity-of-hollow-knights-combat-a60f820fd433)). Knockback on both parties makes every hit physical; brief i-frames plus hit-stop make damage legible. **For a light-combat game, what transfers is not the combat — it's the ideas**: (a) one multipurpose verb reused across contexts, (b) a resource earned by engaging with the world and spent on safety, (c) impact feedback (hit-stop, shake, knockback) on *any* forceful interaction, combat or not. Charms matter mostly as **buildcraft/expression**, which can exist without combat (movement and navigation charms like compass, Dashmaster, Sprintmaster are among the most-used).

### 4. Atmosphere & storytelling
Hollow Knight's melancholy comes from **ruin + evidence of former life + one stubborn cheerful survivor**: giant shells and bones in the background, opaque lore tablets, sparse NPCs who are "walking symptoms of the crumbled order," and Cornifer humming "amidst the end of the world" ([Walking Through Ruin](https://medium.com/@anknguyen21/walking-through-ruin-story-space-and-cyclical-tragedy-in-hollow-knight-3bcaac4e53f6), [Long River Review](https://longriverreview.com/blog/2024/hollow-knight-memory-and-minimalist-storytelling/)). Nothing is explained; the player assembles the story. Crucially the techniques are **medium-independent**: they transfer directly to an empty daylight estate — arguably better, since "abandoned but sunlit and well-kept" is an eerier register than dark ruins. Larkin's music brief ("dark elegance, minimal instrumentation") shows restraint outperforms bombast; Silksong even uses **two-stem tracks that thin out in side rooms** ([Bandcamp Daily interview](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/christopher-larkin-hollow-knight-silksong-interview)).

### 5. Progression & pacing
Benches are the pacing metronome: ~50 of them, each a safe exhale that also saves, heals, sets respawn, and is the *only* place to swap charms and update the map — so benches concentrate all "planning" activity into calm moments ([bench wiki](https://hollowknight.wiki/w/Bench_(Hollow_Knight))). The "one more room" pull is engineered by **always leaving 2–3 visible-but-unreachable things** (a ledge too high, a glinting item, a locked lever seen from the wrong side) and by ability pickups that instantly re-key the whole known world — the moment you get the dash, a dozen remembered gaps light up in memory. Ability cadence in HK is roughly one major traversal power per area for the first two-thirds of the game.

### 6. Silksong: what changed, and reception
Improvements praised across [reviews](https://gameinformer.com/review-in-progress/2025/09/04/hollow-knight-silksong-review-in-progress): **Hornet's faster baseline movement** (sprint, mantling, auto-clamber over small ledges, needle-zip) makes traversal fluid without any pickups; the **Wishes quest board** gives structure to side content with narrative context ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/hollow-knight-silksong-side-quest-design-tracker-journal-changes-good/)); **tools + crests** replace charms with loadout archetypes; **death recovery is kinder** (your cocoon returns cash plus free silk); fast travel unlocks earlier. GMTK found its three-act structure (linear → fully open → hybrid) lets casual players mainline while explorers sequence-break. Criticisms: **runbacks to bosses waste time** ([Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/hollow-knight-silksong-frustration-stems-mostly-from-time-wasting-design/), [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/silksong-problems-runback-criticism/)); the rosary/shard economy adds busywork and "punishes players who use tools too much"; GMTK argues **corpse-running is wrong for a metroidvania at all** because it discourages exploring into the unknown — exactly the behavior the genre needs.

## Ranked top 10: what makes Hollow Knight fun — and what The Barbican should do

**1. Getting lost is a designed pleasure, with player-tunable guidance.**
The map is earned piecewise (find Cornifer → buy map → buy quill → equip compass at a cost), so every player picks their own point on the guided↔blind spectrum, and map knowledge survives death so being lost always converts into permanent progress ([SUPERJUMP](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/getting-lost-by-design-in-hollow-knight/)). Team Cherry's rule: guide "a little bit, and that's enough" ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/action/hollow-knight-silksong-devs-believe-the-real-world-generally-underestimates-players-but-team-cherry-trusts-fans-to-engage-and-figure-the-metroidvania-out-you-can-guide-them-a-little-bit-and-thats-enough/)).
*Barbican:* You already have a better-than-Cornifer device — **the Yellow Line IS your Cornifer**. Make guidance itself the collectible dial: the Tab map only fills in rooms where you've *walked the painted line end-to-end* (line-following literally draws the map, in estate-signage style); rooms crossed off-line show as grey outlines. Later, when the line lies, the map inherits its lies — the map shows where the line *says* the room connects, not where it does. Cheap to build (per-room booleans + a "line integrity" flag already planned) and it fuses the signature mechanic with the genre's best system.

**2. Wrong turns always pay.**
Getting lost stays fun because dead ends almost never feel wasted: geo caches, charms, lore tablets, a shortcut lever, or at minimum a vista. The map-progress-on-death rule means even a fatal wrong turn yields knowledge ([SUPERJUMP](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/getting-lost-by-design-in-hollow-knight/), [Boss Keys](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7ITtPPE-pXE)).
*Barbican:* Adopt as a hard level-design rule for the Arts Centre slice: **every off-line branch terminates in one of: a journal page, a shortcut back, a keycard/fragment, or a view of a landmark that orients the player**. Cheapest reward type: "residue" collectibles — a resident's note, an estate-management memo, a dropped fob — pure text + one sprite each.

**3. Dense interconnection: shortcuts turn a maze into a home.**
Hallownest's areas connect at multiple points; one-way drops and unlockable levers/lifts collapse long routes once learned, so the world shifts from hostile maze to owned territory ([Boss Keys](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7ITtPPE-pXE)). Silksong doubled down: "even greater emphasis on finding shortcuts" ([reviews](https://gameinformer.com/review-in-progress/2025/09/04/hollow-knight-silksong-review-in-progress)).
*Barbican:* The real estate is *made* of this — highwalks over podium over undercroft over Roman level. Rule for the ~20-room slice: **every room reachable by ≥2 routes once fully opened, and at least 4 one-way-then-openable shortcuts** (a roller shutter opened from behind, a dropped ladder, a propped fire door). The planned one-way shutters already fit; budget them deliberately in the room graph before building (annotate shortcut edges on the graph).

**4. Instant, unmistakable area identity.**
Each biome = one palette + one music theme + one enemy family + one traversal texture; you always know where you are by look and sound alone, which makes a confusing layout navigable by feel ([Music of Hollow Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Hollow_Knight), [Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/christopher-larkin-review)).
*Barbican:* Within one estate you still get free biomes: **highwalks (sky, wind, petals), residential blocks (balcony bands, flower boxes), the Arts Centre interior (theatre dark — the one palette break, Ori-style), car parks/undercroft (sodium orange), Roman/medieval level (torch-lit stone)**. Give each a 1-line audio identity even with procedural WebAudio: wind + distant traffic; fountain trickle; HVAC hum; dripping. One ambient loop per zone is enough — Larkin's brief was explicitly "minimal instrumentation."

**5. Visible-but-unreachable things power the "one more room" pull.**
HK constantly shows you a ledge you can't reach, an item behind a gap, a lever on the far side — and a new ability instantly re-keys every remembered tease. This memory-and-return loop is the metroidvania dopamine core ([Boss Keys](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7ITtPPE-pXE)).
*Barbican:* Concrete quota: **2–3 teases per room** in the slice — a keycard reader glowing red, a hoarding with something glinting through the slats, a grapple anchor across an atrium, a scuffed Yellow Line stub leading into a wall. Because abilities are already planned (dash/barge/grapple/keycards), seed their locks *everywhere* from room one; the pickup then retroactively rewards attention.

**6. Benches: the exhale that structures the breath.**
Benches concentrate save/heal/respawn/charm-swap/map-update into one calm ritual, making them emotional anchors as much as checkpoints; their spacing *is* the difficulty and pacing dial ([bench wiki](https://hollowknight.wiki/w/Bench_(Hollow_Knight)), [community threads](https://steamcommunity.com/app/367520/discussions/0/1471967615853426073/)). Silksong criticism shows the failure mode: benches too far from hard content = time-wasting runbacks ([Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/hollow-knight-silksong-frustration-stems-mostly-from-time-wasting-design/)).
*Barbican:* The planned concrete benches are perfect (the real estate's benches are iconic). Make them do everything at once: save, heal, **and be the only place the map redraws** (see #1) — that last rule is nearly free and creates the "navigate by memory between rests" tension. Place one within ~30 seconds of anything hard; with no bosses yet, this mostly means before gauntlets and the rigging mini-boss.

**7. Trust the player; explain almost nothing.**
No quest markers, no tutorial popups after the first minutes, opaque lore — and players report this as the *reason* discovery feels earned. "Maybe don't worry too much about being a Metroidvania" — Team Cherry avoided genre archetypes that didn't serve discovery ([ACMI](https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/from-ludum-dare-to-pharloom/), [Nintendo AU](https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/the-metamorphosis-of-hollow-knight-with-team-cherry-aussie-developer-interview/)).
*Barbican:* Teach abilities the HK way: a safe room whose geometry only exits via the new move (the Phase 2 gauntlets already do this — keep them wordless; the "pause, text card, flash" pickup moment is enough). Never label the Yellow Line's behavior; when it first forks, say nothing. Players discovering "the line lied to me" unprompted is the City of Tears moment.

**8. Ruin + one cheerful survivor = melancholy that lands.**
The tone comes from evidence of vanished life (shells, bones, empty grand architecture) punctuated by rare, warm NPCs — Cornifer humming, Elderbug waiting — whose smallness against the ruin *creates* the melancholy ([Walking Through Ruin](https://medium.com/@anknguyen21/walking-through-ruin-story-space-and-cyclical-tragedy-in-hollow-knight-3bcaac4e53f6), [Long River Review](https://longriverreview.com/blog/2024/hollow-knight-memory-and-minimalist-storytelling/)).
*Barbican:* An empty estate in bright sunshine is *more* uncanny than a dark one — lean in with evidence of interrupted life: washing still on balcony lines, a kettle mid-boil audible through a door, today's newspaper on a bench, the flower boxes freshly watered by no one. Budget **exactly one NPC for the slice**: a groundskeeper who keeps repainting the Yellow Line ("someone has to keep it fresh, don't they?") — the Cornifer, the lore source, and an in-fiction explanation for why the line changes. Audible before visible (whistling, brush sounds) so finding them is itself a navigation game.

**9. One multipurpose verb; feedback that makes physics feel physical.**
Pogo works because one input is attack + escape + platforming at once, and every impact carries hit-stop, knockback, and shake ([combat analysis](https://medium.com/@roryhoeschen/the-hypnotizing-simplicity-of-hollow-knights-combat-a60f820fd433)). The soul system ties resource gain to engagement. With light combat, the transferable core is verb-reuse and impact feedback, not the nail.
*Barbican:* Make the **barge** the pogo-equivalent multipurpose verb: breaks hoardings, kills the cleaning machine, slams stuck doors, knocks levers, and gives a small horizontal boost usable mid-platforming (barge-extend across a gap). One move, five contexts. Keep Celeste-grade feedback discipline (squash/stretch exists; hit-stop/screen shake planned) on *every* forceful interaction. Skip a soul meter entirely.

**10. Buildcraft and structured side content personalize the run (Silksong's lesson).**
Charms → Silksong's crests/tools let players lean into traversal, safety, or aggression; the Wishes board made side content legible with narrative context, and faster baseline movement made pure traversal feel great before any pickup ([GameRant](https://gamerant.com/hollow-knight-silksong-side-quest-design-tracker-journal-changes-good/), [GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-world-design-of-hollow-knight)).
*Barbican:* Small-scope version: **3–5 "estate notices"** on a real notice board in a lobby (diegetic quest board, styled like laminated resident notices: "LOST CAT — last seen Gilbert Bridge level 3", "Water outage – Frobisher Crescent") that double as navigation puzzles using estate place-names. And 2–3 equippable "pocket items" instead of charms: a compass keyring (shows position on map), worn plimsolls (slightly longer dash), a resident's diary (marks scuffed-line secrets walked past). Cap it there.

## Do NOT copy

- **Corpse-running / currency loss on death.** GMTK's verdict: it "doesn't work for a Metroidvania" because it punishes venturing into the unknown; it was Silksong's most-criticized system ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-world-design-of-hollow-knight), [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/silksong-problems-runback-criticism/)). The <0.5s respawn-at-entry plan is right — keep death costless except position.
- **Long boss runbacks.** The single loudest Silksong complaint ([Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/hollow-knight-silksong-frustration-stems-mostly-from-time-wasting-design/)). Put a bench directly before the Stage Manager.
- **HK's combat depth and difficulty curve.** Without a nail-equivalent, don't bolt on health-sponge enemies, parries, or a soul economy; the game's challenge should live in navigation, platforming, and the line's deceptions.
- **Darkness as atmosphere.** HK's gloom is inseparable from its caves; the settled daylight direction is the differentiator. Reserve dark for the theatre interior and Roman level so it reads as a palette *break* (the Ori reference), not a default.
- **Scale.** 40+ hours, 15 biomes, 40 bosses — HK's density-per-room is the copyable thing, not its size. A 20-room slice with HK-grade interconnection beats 60 thin rooms.
- **Consumable-gated tools / farming economies.** Silksong's shard economy read as "busywork" and a farming timer ([Steam discussion breakdown](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1030300/discussions/0/595159519773913302/)). Abilities in The Barbican should be permanent and free to use.
- **Hiding the map system itself too aggressively.** Some players find even Cornifer-hunting tedious ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/367520/discussions/0/682991471067001204/)). Since the map fills via line-walking (#1), the *acquisition* friction is already gone — don't add a second layer of map-gating on top.
