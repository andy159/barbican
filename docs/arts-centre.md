# The Arts Centre — Research Notes (Phase 6 vertical slice)

Research on the real Barbican Centre interiors for the "Arts Centre" level: foyers,
Barbican Theatre, Martini Bar, Cinema 1, and the film playing inside it (Stan
Brakhage's *Mothlight*, 1963). Prose per venue with sources, then GAME NOTES
(palettes, prop lists, signature details, Mothlight procedural animation spec).

---

## 1. Foyers & circulation

The foyers are the connective tissue of the whole centre: four public floors
(signed **Level -2 up to Level 3**, with the lakeside/ground foyer in the middle)
linking theatre, concert hall, gallery, cinema, library, shops and cafes. The
architecture is Chamberlin, Powell & Bon at full strength — **massive circular
pick-hammered concrete columns** (the ridged, exposed-aggregate finish made by
hand-tooling the cast surface), **deep square ceiling coffers**, each coffer
originally carrying a **globe light**, wooden-block floors in places, and
**brass/bronze handrails and door furniture** set against the raw concrete. The
lakeside foyer sits sunken below the podium level, opening through a glazed wall
onto the lakeside terrace with its fountains; heavy manual doors on that edge are
a long-running complaint in the current Renewal consultation. The famous
**rust-orange/brown carpet** runs through the foyer levels — a warm 1970s tone
against grey concrete that has become part of the centre's identity. Potted
**ficus (weeping fig) trees** and other planting soften the concrete indoors,
echoing the conservatory built above the theatre flytower.

In the **2006 AHMM refurbishment**, an internal bridge was added linking the Silk
Street entrance foyer to the lakeside foyer, and Cartlidge Levene + Studio
Myerscough designed the **wayfinding system**: vivid **orange** panels and
supergraphics — some floor-to-ceiling lowercase lettering — in **Futura SH**
across seven floors, deliberately clashing with the grey concrete. Big orange
level numerals ("-2", "G", "1", "2", "3") and orange directional totems are the
single most copyable visual after the concrete itself.

## 2. Barbican Theatre

Built as the London home of the Royal Shakespeare Company; Peter Hall and
designer John Bury consulted on the auditorium. **1,156 seats** in a wide fan on
a steep rake with generous legroom, and — the signature — **no aisles at all**:
every row runs unbroken wall to wall, and each row is entered through its own
pair of doors in the side walls. The doors **close automatically (magnetically)
as the house goes down** — famously triggered together on the stage manager's
cue, a soft synchronized *thunk* down both walls just before curtain. The room
is dark and warm: dark timber facings and balcony fronts, low ceiling planes
stepping toward the proscenium, seats in a deep red/burgundy against the wood.

Technical: the **flytower is 33 m — one of the tallest in Europe** — and so ugly
from the flats above that the **conservatory was built specifically to hide it**.
The flying system is fully automated (no manual counterweights); a 2013 £3.2 m
Theatreplan refurbishment replaced the entire grid with a rotating-plank
load-bearing floor and movable point hoists. The **safety curtain is stainless
steel and meets in the middle**: a lower half rising from the stage floor like a
barrier and an upper half descending from the flytower. The stage has a large
acting area, big wing space, and a **forestage/orchestra pit** on lifts between
stage and front row.

## 3. Martini Bar

A small cocktail bar on the **upper foyer level (Level 1)**, on the balcony
overlooking the lakeside foyer and, through the glazing, the lake and terrace.
Created in **2012** to accompany the *Designing 007* Bond exhibition and kept on
permanently. Dark and atmospheric against the daylight foyer: a sculptural bar
structure by designer **Omer Abel**, back-bar shelving lined with spirits and
glassware, and — after Witherford Watson Mann's foyer refurbishment — modernist
furniture (Alvar Aalto, Hans Wegner, Robin Day, Jasper Morrison pieces) chosen
to suit the listed building. Classic martinis, champagne; evening opening
(5–10:30 pm) with a 5–6 pm happy hour, so in fiction it reads as a place that
only wakes after dark. City AM called the mix roughly: espresso, small-batch
vodka, brutalist architecture and high art.

## 4. Cinema 1

Cinema 1 is inside the main building, reached from the foyers and signed at
**Level -2** — the **deepest point of the centre, about 17 feet below sea
level** ("dig deep as well as build high"). Roughly **280 seats** on a strikingly
**steep rake, with stepped stairs down both sides — likened to football
terraces** — so every seat clears the head in front. Chamberlin, Powell & Bon's
1969 drawings even toyed with projecting films on the ceiling for reclining
viewers before sanity (and spilled drinks) prevailed. Originally one of three
screens here; Cinemas 2 & 3 moved to a separate Beech Street building in 2012
and this auditorium became "Cinema 1". Recent RUFF Architects refurbishment
relined the auditorium walls in a recycled-polyester acoustic fibre and kept the
brutalist shell legible. Dark room, dark seats (deep red/oxblood reads correctly
in pixel art), a wide screen with **motorised black masking** that adjusts to
aspect ratio, and both 35mm and digital projection from a rear booth — it hosts
festivals, silent film with live scores, and archive programmes, exactly the
sort of place that would screen Brakhage.

## 5. *Mothlight* (Stan Brakhage, 1963)

A **cameraless** 16mm film, **3 min 13 s, silent**. Brakhage pressed **moth
wings, flower petals, blades of grass, leaves, seeds and other detritus**
between two strips of clear perforated splicing tape and printed the result.
On screen: a **bright white field** (clear film + projector lamp) across which
**translucent organic silhouettes flicker** — brown/sepia/ochre wing fragments
with visible vein patterns like leaf skeletons, ragged leaf edges, grass blades
as thin diagonal strokes. Because each frame's contents bear no relation to the
last, nothing "moves" — the image **replaces itself entirely up to 24 times a
second**, reading as a nervous stutter. Density surges and collapses: bursts of
near-black cluttered frames, then several almost blank white frames, then a
single centered wing. Occasional greens (grass) and dusty pinks (petals) break
the brown range. Brakhage's gloss: what a moth might see from birth to death if
black were white and white were black.

## Signature details (things Barbican regulars will recognise)

**Foyers**
1. The orange **Level -2…3 supergraphics** in lowercase Futura — floor-to-ceiling numerals on bare concrete.
2. **Coffered ceiling with one glowing globe per coffer**, receding in perspective rows.
3. The **rust-orange carpet meeting pick-hammered concrete** — softest and hardest materials in one seam (plus a ficus in a concrete drum).

**Theatre**
1. **Every row has its own doors, and they all close at once** on the SM's cue — animate a wave of doors thunking shut down the wall as the level's "point of no return".
2. The **two-part steel safety curtain**, bottom half rising out of the floor to meet the top half.
3. The **flytower is hidden under the conservatory** — climbing it, you burst out among tropical plants and glass.

**Martini Bar**
1. It only exists because of a **James Bond exhibition (2012)** and never left — a Bond-ish glint on the shaker.
2. **Aalto/Wegner/Robin Day chairs** — licensed-modernism furniture as set dressing.
3. A dark, brass-lit room **overlooking the daylight foyer void** — inverted lighting from everything around it.

**Cinema 1**
1. It's **below sea level** — the deepest room in the estate; perfect above the Roman wall in game fiction.
2. The **football-terrace rake** with stepped side channels and tread lights.
3. Chamberlin, Powell & Bon once proposed **projecting films on the ceiling** for reclining viewers — a journal/easter-egg gag.

## Mothlight — procedural animation spec

Render the screen as a bright `#f2ede2` field at cinema-lamp brightness (let it
be the dominant light source, flickering the whole room's ambient tint).
Maintain a pool of 6–10 organic silhouette stamps: a veined moth wing (rounded
triangle with 1px dark rib lines radiating from one corner), a ragged leaf
(blob with notched edge and midrib), 2–3 thin grass blades (1px diagonal
strokes), a petal (soft oval), and a seed-cluster speckle patch. Draw them in
2–3 translucent layers of ochre `#b98a3c`, sepia `#7d5a33`, umber `#3b2a1a`,
with rare grass-green `#6b6f3d` or petal-pink `#c99a8a` accents; where stamps
overlap, darken toward umber (multiply-ish). Crucially, nothing tweens:
**replace the entire composition every 2–4 frames at 60Hz** (i.e. 15–30 changes
a second) with fresh random stamps, positions, rotations and scales — no
continuity between compositions. Drive density with a slow random walk (period
~2–5 s) between three states: sparse (0–2 stamps, mostly white), normal (3–6),
and burst (8–12, frame goes nearly dark) — and insert occasional runs of 3–8
pure-white frames after a burst. Add faint vertical scratch lines and 1px dust
specks that also change per frame, plus a ±3% brightness flicker on the white
field. Loop nothing; run ~3 minutes then a white tail and the take-up flap.
The film is silent — the only audio is the projector: a 24Hz mechanical purr.
